About

MDMC is a small team split across Christchurch, Sydney, and Yokohama. We work with founders, operators, and in-house teams on the design problems that don't fit neatly into a brief — the ones that touch product, brand, and operations at the same time.

What we do

Branding & identity, digital product design, web & platform design, print & collateral, and the kind of experience design that starts with fixing what's actually broken. We work end-to-end — from diagnosis through to design, build, and beyond.

How we work

Two-week loops, no layers between you and the people doing the work. We ask more questions upfront than most, because the right solution usually isn't the obvious one.

Who we work with

Small and medium businesses that have outgrown their current setup, and larger organisations that need an outside eye on how their customers truly experience them. Hospitality, retail, professional services, and anyone whose digital presence no longer reflects the quality of what they do.

What we don't do

Spec work, or projects without a named decision maker. We also never make something we wouldn't want to use ourselves.

MDMC Opens Its Yokohama Studio

After two years of working with Japanese clients from Christchurch and Sydney, MDMC has opened a permanent studio in Yokohama — our third, and our first in the northern hemisphere.

The studio sits a few minutes from Yokohama Station, shared with a small photography collective. It will be home to our bilingual design and production work, led day-to-day by the team that built the Zenrise platform.

Japan has been part of MDMC's work almost from the beginning — first through translation-adjacent design projects, then through full brand and web engagements. A local studio means local hours, local conversations, and fewer 6am calls for everyone involved.

The Yokohama studio opens with two full-time staff and room for four. We're hiring — a bilingual administrator first, with design roles to follow later in the year.

If you're in the area, come by. If you'd like to work with us in Japan — on brand, web, or something that doesn't fit neatly into either — get in touch.

Overview

While picking up the phone is easy for many, for others, it's an obstacle between them and the help they need. With employee assistance program provider OCP aware of this issue, we suggested that an overhaul of their existing appointment system was necessary to connect as many workers as possible with the support they deserve.

Available on mobile and desktop, this service has revolutionised OCP's appointment system and prioritised accessibility — users only need to follow three simple steps online to get the support they need.

We worked alongside OCP's clinical and operations teams from first workshop to launch — mapping how appointments were actually made, where people abandoned the process, and what a counsellor genuinely needs to know before a first session.

Challenge

Counselling is a sensitive first contact: every additional field, login, or phone call is a reason to give up. The booking flow had to satisfy clinical scheduling rules and employer confidentiality at the same time, without ever feeling like an intake form.

The existing phone-only process also disappeared outside business hours — precisely when many people finally have the privacy to seek help. Whatever replaced it had to be available at midnight and feel as safe as a conversation.

Approach

We reduced the entire booking to reason, time, and contact details — with no sign-up wall in front of it. Everything a counsellor needs is collected after the appointment is secured.

Each step fits on one screen and asks for one decision. Progress is explicit, nothing is asked twice, and going back never loses an answer.

Availability is generated from counsellor calendars, session length, and travel buffers, so a bookable slot is always a real slot. Behind the calm interface sits a rules engine: urgency triage, location matching and counsellor specialisation all resolve before a time is ever offered, so no one is asked to choose something that can't be honoured.

Employers see utilisation, never identities. The interface says so plainly at the moment of booking, which is where the doubt actually sits — the privacy statement is written in plain language and sits beside the confirm button, not behind a link.

Outcome

Within three months of launch, the majority of OCP appointments were being booked online rather than by phone — including outside business hours, when no phone line was ever available.

"People who would never have called us are now booking at ten at night. That is the whole point of the service."

Bilingual Administrator

About the role

MDMC Japan is looking for a part-time bilingual administrator to join our Yokohama studio from December 2026. You will be the operational heart of the studio: the person who keeps client correspondence moving, the calendar honest, and the paperwork done properly in both Japanese and English.

Day to day, that means handling correspondence with clients and suppliers in Japanese and English, coordinating meetings across our three studios and their time zones, preparing invoices and quotations, supporting administrative filings, and keeping the studio itself organised and welcoming.

You should have native-level Japanese and confident business English, experience running administration in a small office or studio, and comfort with standard tools for scheduling, documents, and light bookkeeping. Experience with a design or creative business is welcome but not required — care and clarity matter more.

The role is part-time, two to three days per week at our Kitanaka (Bashamichi) office in Yokohama, with some flexibility in which days. Compensation is hourly, set by experience. We expect to welcome the successful candidate around December 2026.
